The Ritchie Ledges Carvings

FIELD RECORD001
CLASSIFICATIONFIELD RECORD — DOCUMENTED REAL-WORLD FIND
LOCATIONRitchie Ledges, Cuyahoga Valley National Park — Peninsula, Ohio
DATE OF RECOVERY14 November 2026
RECORDED BYAngelo Padula — founder, in the field
STATUSDOCUMENTED IN SITU — LEFT EXACTLY AS FOUND

On 14 November 2026, during the filming of a walkthrough of the Ledges Trail, an unmarked passage near Ice Box Cave gave up something most visitors walk past without ever knowing it exists: faces, cut into the sandstone by hand, watching the trail from the rock.

WHAT THE STONE HOLDS

The passage holds four carvings. Two finished human heads. A third head, begun and never completed — the work simply stops. And lower, near ground level where a passerby would never think to look, a horse.

WHAT IS KNOWN

The carvings date to the 1800s. Local accounts attribute them to a farmer who worked this land; the name most often attached is William Geoppert, a landowner here in the late 1800s. That attribution is rumored, not proven. Who the faces depict is recorded nowhere.

The Archive reports them exactly that way: documented where documentation exists, open where it does not.

“I've lived here for over twenty years and I never knew this was here.”
— FIELD NOTE, RECORDED ON SITE, 14 NOVEMBER 2026
